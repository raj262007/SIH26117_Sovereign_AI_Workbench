AMERICAN SOCIETY OF MECHANICAL ENGINEERS - ASME CODE FOR PRESSURE PIPING
ASME B31.3: Process Piping Design and Structural Integrity Code

CHAPTER II: DESIGN OF PIPING COMPONENTS UNDER PRESSURE
Paragraph 304.1: Straight Pipe Under Internal Pressure
The required thickness of straight pipe sections subjected to internal pressure shall not be less than the design minimum thickness plus corrosion and mechanical allowances:
    t_m = t + c
Where:
    t_m = Total minimum required thickness including all allowances (mm).
    t = Pressure design thickness (mm).
    c = Sum of mechanical allowances (thread depth or groove depth) plus corrosion and erosion allowance.

Paragraph 304.1.2: Pressure Design Thickness Formula
For pipe with t < D/6, the internal pressure design thickness for cylindrical pipes is given by:
    t = (P * D) / (2 * (S * E * W + P * Y))
Where:
    P = Internal design gauge pressure (MPa).
    D = Outside diameter of pipe (mm).
    S = Basic allowable stress value for material from Table A-1 (MPa).
    E = Quality factor from Table A-1A or Table A-1B (1.00 for seamless CS).
    W = Weld joint strength reduction factor per Para 302.3.5(e).
    Y = Material and temperature coefficient (0.4 for carbon steel up to 482°C / 900°F).

CHAPTER III: MATERIALS AND DESIGN STRESS LIMITS
Paragraph 302.3.5: Limits of Calculated Stresses Due to Sustained Loads
The sum of external axial stresses produced by sustained loads such as internal pressure, weight of pipe and contents, and external insulation must not exceed the basic allowable stress (S_h) at maximum metal temperature:
    S_L <= S_h
For ASTM A106 Grade B carbon steel, allowable tensile stress S_h is 20.0 ksi (137.9 MPa) between -29°C and 204°C.

CHAPTER V: FABRICATION, ASSEMBLY, AND TESTING
Paragraph 341.4: Extent of Required Non-Destructive Examination
Components carrying Category M fluid (hazardous hydrocarbons, sour gas) require 100% volumetric and ultrasonic examination of circumferential butt welds and high-strain branch fittings. Any measured wall thinning below the structural integrity design envelope requires immediate formal engineering disposition.
